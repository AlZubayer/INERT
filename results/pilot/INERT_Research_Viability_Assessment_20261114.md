# INERT research viability assessment — offline owner decision aid

## 1. Bottom-line recommendation

**Preferred next action: Option C — pause the frozen pilot and, only if the owner wants to invest further, authorize a narrowly scoped interface and execution-prediction study followed by a new preregistration.** The present evidence does not justify automatically spending more GPU time on WM-A P0 or another WM-C prompt repair. WM-B is operationally usable but has not demonstrated enough baseline discrimination on the three-task engineering sample to make the planned KT-A and KT-C endpoints credible yet. A focused study should establish whether a local model can reliably predict *failing* test cases under a stable, parseable interface before any new scientific scoring. This is an **inferred research recommendation**, not a Section 11.6 pilot verdict. The owner may instead choose Option D if that engineering investment has low value.

## 4. WM-A feasibility findings

**Calculated from the 62 original completions:** 9 parsed; 53 failed (85.48%). Channel C: 1/31 parsed, 30/31 failed; Channel D: 8/31 parsed, 23/31 failed. Exactly 61/62 finished at the 1,024-token `length` limit (98.39%); one stopped at 961 tokens. All 53 parse failures were length-limited with no parseable `RESULT: k/n`; zero contained a wrong-denominator or otherwise invalid numeric result. Eight of the nine accepted parses came from an **unterminated `<think>` span** at the length limit: a `RESULT: 3/3` appeared in unfinished reasoning, so the frozen parser accepted the last numeric match. Only one accepted result was a completed response outside a closed reasoning span. This is an observable format/provenance risk, not a change to the parser. All nine accepted predictions were `3/3`; one was wrong against its candidate's recorded `2/3` Docker truth. See `data/p0/viability_assessment_20261114/audit.json`, original cache key-to-item mappings, and `data/candidates.jsonl`.

**Observable output pattern:** 61/62 outputs opened `<think>` without closing it; each raw output has a distinct hash. Several repeat local phrases such as “Wait” while reconsidering tests, and raw tails often end mid-analysis. There is no exact duplicated raw completion. Accepted result positions are calls **20, 22, 26, 29, 31, 34, 35, 36, 42**; none occurred in calls 1–19 or 43–62. This cluster mixes task, condition, channel, and session effects, so it is not evidence of a temporal improvement. The first 18 calls averaged 95.77 s and the following 44 averaged 103.02 s, but the task sequence changed at the same time. The 1,024-token budget is visibly spent mostly in unfinished reasoning; the internal cause is unknown.

**Calculated for the 61 calls with original live VRAM observations:** median HTTP latency **101.43 s**, NumPy linear-interpolation P90 **106.12 s**, 62,401 completion tokens over 6,150.21 HTTP seconds = **10.15 tokens/s**, and sampled peak **15,926 MiB**. For all 62 original cached HTTP latencies, median is 101.73 s, P10 94.50 s, P90 106.35 s; summed HTTP time is 6,256.58 s. Two original server starts took 26.24 s and 28.23 s; the separate replay start took 25.22 s. Startup is not part of HTTP latency. Original call 62 took 106.37 s; the isolated replay took 96.56 s, a 9.81 s difference under the same request identity. The cause of the timing difference is unknown. Q4 loaded successfully, but its peak leaves roughly 385 MiB between the sampled 15,926 MiB and the reported 16,311 MiB card total; a sampled peak does not prove a safe margin for a longer completion. Q3 has **not** been downloaded or tested. Sources: original measurement, `data/p0/load_attempts.json`, and replay summary.

**Sharp mathematical bound for the *first* 1,024-token pass:** with 62 fixed latencies and 38 unknown nonnegative latencies, a completed 100-call median (average of order positions 50 and 51) lies between the mean of the observed 12th/13th smallest latencies, **95.10 s**, and the mean of the observed 50th/51st, **104.77 s**. These endpoints are attainable as limits by placing all missing calls below or above the observed set. Thus the completed first-pass median would necessarily exceed the frozen 50-second *drop-WM-A timing band*, if its existing records were kept. **This is not an official P0 decision**: the design's escalation changes the output budget and the final trial population. No corresponding bound exists for the unrun 2,048-token pass.

The 53 observed length-limited, result-free outputs already ensure at least **53/100 = 53%** first-pass truncation after the remaining 38 calls, above the registered **5%** escalation trigger. Escalation is therefore inevitable if the first pass is completed unchanged. Its parse rate, latency, VRAM, and 10% parse-gate outcome are unknown. More tokens might allow final answers, but may lengthen calls, reach the configured 300-second request timeout, or increase memory use. It may also leave most responses unparseable. **No `P0_PARSE_FAILURE_STOP`, `full`, `reduced`, or `drop_wm_a` status is assigned from partial data.** The separate first-200 pilot-scoring checkpoint was not reached.

## 6. WM-B capability and validity risks

**Independently recalculated from** `data/smoke/diagnostics/wm_b_engineering_smoke_20261114/scores/wm_b.jsonl`, `candidates.jsonl`, and `manifest.json`: 198/198 assigned core scores (99/channel, 11/condition/channel), 0 parse failures, 68/198 exact passing-test counts (C 29/99; D 39/99). The three-task T0 balanced accuracies are **C 0.5833** and **D 0.5000**; among six failing T0 candidates, **C predicted pass for 5**, **D for all 6**. The 198 assignments correspond to 194 distinct live requests plus four exact cache hits. There are 11 core candidates across three tasks. This was an **engineering-only** local substitution, not official smoke or pilot scoring.

**Inference:** Format compliance does not establish execution-prediction competence. With a T0 false-pass rate of 5/6 in C, a fixed paired sample has only 1/6 (16.7 percentage points) of possible upward false-pass movement; in D, 6/6 leaves no upward room on those six cases. That saturation directly threatens KT-A's *failing-candidate* primary endpoint, even though the diagnostic's passing-candidate mirror showed claim-linked false-fail flips. D's 0.5000 is below the frozen 0.55 validity floor **for this engineering sample only**. A 50-task sample could differ. WM-B could still support a meaningful inert-text test if its full pilot T0 accuracy and failing-case ranking are adequate; current evidence does not establish either. Without WM-A, the frozen design permits a best possible KT-A verdict of `PASS`, never `STRONG_PASS`, and loses the purpose-built versus general-model comparison.

Five C failures reached length at 1,024 without a valid result, one C response ended with a wrong last denominator `1/1`, and two D responses said `0/10` for three-test tasks. The archived **diagnostic-only** prompt comparison independently recomputes the frozen parser over `data/smoke/diagnostics/prompt_adaptation_20261113/responses/wm_c.json`: C0 58/99 failures (37 wrong denominators; 82 length finishes), C1/C2 each 99/99 failures (all length finishes), D0 64/99 failures (all wrong denominators), D1 42/99, and D2 50/99 (both all wrong denominators). Source: [Prompt Adaptation Diagnostic](Prompt_Adaptation_Diagnostic_20261113.md) and `data/p0/viability_assessment_20261114/wm_c_diagnostic.json`.

**Inference:** A further *formally approved* D adaptation could be technically motivated by D1's relative gain, but none of the tested variants met the gate, C remains poor, and D's accepted all-pass responses do not establish useful prediction. Given the weak-tier role and the risk of post-hoc tuning, another WM-C repair is not the preferred next spend. WM-C remains **unavailable for official scientific scoring under the current smoke gate**; it is not silently dropped from the frozen design.

## 8. Implications for KT-A, KT-B, and KT-C

**Prepared input counts, not model results:** The 50 active tasks have 549 active candidate records; 182 are core (95 passing, 87 failing), with 32 tasks supplying four and 18 supplying three core candidates. This is below the nominal 200 core target but retains at least one passing and one failing candidate per task. Natural text is nonempty for **139/182 core candidates** and **494/549 active candidates**; 139/182 generated Channel-D thoughts are nonempty according to `data/p0/preparation_audit_20261114/final_input_audit.json`. The item file has 3,276 KT-A/B items and 1,020 items per KT-C rung; each rung spans **510 distinct candidates** across 50 tasks (mean 10.2, range 2–16 candidates/task). Sources: `data/tasks.jsonl`, `data/candidates.jsonl`, `data/items.jsonl`, `data/thoughts.jsonl`, and `data/p0/viability_assessment_20261114/wm_b_and_inputs.json`.

- **KT-A (effect, RQ1):** The paired neutral controls can distinguish misleading claims from benign wording *if* a WM-channel has valid T0 discrimination and parse coverage. The observed WM-B failing-case all-pass tendency constrains the available effect scale; WM-A/WM-C parsing removes many pairs. No registered endpoint or bootstrap interval exists yet.
- **KT-B (stripping cost):** Nonempty natural text is available in a substantial share of core candidates, so the comparison is technically feasible. Its interpretation still requires a competent T0 baseline and parsed pairs; a weak model can show an apparent benefit from claims without accurately simulating test execution. No `S_nat` or verdict is available.
- **KT-C (selection, RQ2):** Poor exact counts imply miscalibration, but **do not by themselves prove selection regret**. If scores are mostly tied at pass, ranking discrimination and claim enrichment may be weak; text-induced shifts could nevertheless change selection. Regret requires paired, executed rewards across the actual candidate pools, with task-clustered uncertainty. The design runs KT-C only after KT-A is not `KILL`; this stage gate remains untested.

## 11. Recommended owner decision

Authorize **no further inference now**. Decide whether INERT's question merits a bounded, newly preregistered Option C interface study with explicit success criteria for baseline failing-case discrimination, parsing, and resource use. If yes, specify and approve that protocol before any calls. If no, choose Option D and retain this pilot as an incomplete feasibility record. Completing WM-A P0 is mathematically unlikely to rescue the *first-pass* timing band and would almost certainly trigger the expensive escalation; its final parse and latency are unknown, so A remains a legitimate but lower-priority scientific choice. A WM-B-centered pilot is plausible only conditionally: 50-task baseline validity and ranking must be established under a separately approved deviation, and its general-model evidence cannot substitute for a purpose-built WM. Another WM-C adaptation is not justified as the next default action. **There is currently no demonstrated credible path to both RQ1 and RQ2 under the frozen interface**, though a new interface study could establish one. These are owner choices about future work, not frozen scientific verdicts.

## 12. Limitations and unknowns

**Unknown:** 2,048-token WM-A behavior; Q3 loading/performance; final 50-task WM-B baseline validity; full task-level KT-A/B/C effects; whether failures are caused mainly by model capability, prompt assembly, or the burden of tracing Python assertions. Runtime scenarios are extrapolations, not confidence intervals. The original call-62 VRAM remains missing. The three-task diagnostics are dependent and selected, and the WM-A P0 assignments are the first 62 in registered order rather than a random 62-call sample. An accepted match within unfinished reasoning may be a correct forecast or a transient thought; no unrun trial can resolve it retrospectively. No external academic claim is needed for this decision aid.
